# Phase 6 cancellation risk: July and August 2024

Generated by `python -m flightops report`. Don't edit this file. Tables come from the database, and the SQL behind each one sits under it. Times are UTC.

An inbound is a flown leg on a tail that touched ATL that month, with an arrival delay (diverted legs have none). Its targets are the tail's next three legs by scheduled departure, flown or cancelled, scheduled within 24h of the inbound's arrival. Each target gets the July cancellation rate of its cell: carrier × rank × delay bucket × carrier stress, or the all-carrier cell when the carrier cell has fewer than 200 samples. Carrier stress is the cancelled share of the carrier's national departures scheduled in the three whole UTC hours before the inbound's actual departure hour, excluding the inbound's own tail. A cancellation counts as known from its scheduled departure. Rates and the flag threshold are fitted on July. `debby` (August 2-6) and `august` are the holdout.

## Findings

- **The rate table beats both baselines in every period and rank, but by little on normal days.** On `august` at rank 1, its Brier score is 0.00448, against 0.00475 for delay alone and 0.00498 for the carrier's constant rate, 6% and 10% lower. By rank 3 the lead over delay alone is 3% (0.00678 against 0.00700). On `debby` it is 4% at rank 1 (0.03954 against 0.04126). The big gain is inside July's outage, 16% at rank 1 (0.04493 against 0.05340), and that period is in the fit set.
- **Stress is the feature that carries it.** In July, pairs under 2% stress were cancelled 0.67% of the time, 2-10% at 2.65%, and 10%+ at 15.68%. Delay alone can't separate a long delay on a quiet day from one on a bad day.
- **On `august` the model overpredicts.** From the second decile up, predicted rates are above actual, and in the top decile it predicts 4.25% against 2.58% actual. August's pairs were cancelled at 0.57%, about half July's normal days (1.08%), and the rates carry July's level.
- **On `debby` the model underpredicts.** In deciles 3 to 9, actual rates are 1.6x to 5.4x predicted (decile 3: 0.41% predicted, 2.20% actual). A weather day at the hub cancelled more than carrier stress and delay alone imply. The top decile is close: 13.00% against 14.55%.
- **The flag is an outage detector.** The July threshold is 18.3%, with F1 0.336 (29.8% precision, 38.6% recall). It is set by the outage: there it catches 71-74% of cancellations at 28-33% precision, but on July's normal days and on `august` it catches 2-4%, and on `debby` 5-7%, at 18-24% precision. On an ordinary bad day it is almost silent.
- **Carrier cells almost always have enough samples.** 99.0% of `august` pairs and 96.1% of `debby` pairs are scored from the carrier's own cell. No pair in any period lacks a July cell.
- **N102DN is flagged where `propagate` saw nothing.** After its 316-min DEN→ATL, with Delta's stress at 49.6%, the three cancelled legs score 49.5%, 51.4% and 46.7%, from Delta cells of 1,309, 1,276 and 1,197 samples. All three are above the threshold and all three were cancelled. BTS lists the tail on two flights at 02:50, ATL→IAH (cancelled) and ATL→BWI (flown), so ATL→BWI is rank 5 and unscored.

## Limits

- **August tests the high-stress cells only partly.** Debby puts 27,120 pairs in the 10%+ bucket, 3,176 of them cancelled, so that cell is tested out of sample. But most of Debby's are at 10-30% stress: at 30%+ it has 2,765 pairs against the outage's 19,927. The outage-level cells, and the threshold they set, come from a single July event and are untested out of sample.
- **The delay feature includes en-route change after the decision moment.** The decision moment is the inbound's actual departure, but the feature is its arrival delay, as in `propagate`.
- **Cancellations count as known from their scheduled departure.** BTS gives no announcement time, so a cancellation announced hours ahead counts only when it was due to leave.
- **The stress window is whole UTC hours.** It is the three clock hours before the hour of the inbound's departure, so it ends 0-59 min before the decision moment and misses the latest cancellations.
- **The schedule after the inbound is partly hindsight.** BTS records the tail that flew or was assigned, so a tail swap is invisible, and one tail can appear on two flights at once, as N102DN does.

## Flag threshold

Chosen on all of July, ranks 1-3 pooled, as the predicted rate that maximizes F1 when every pair at or above it is flagged.

| threshold_pct | flagged | flagged_cancelled | precision_pct | recall_pct | f1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 18.3 | 26156 | 7796 | 29.8 | 38.6 | 0.336 |

<details><summary>SQL</summary>

```sql
WITH th AS (SELECT r FROM (
    SELECT r, 2.0 * sum(c) OVER w / (sum(n) OVER w + sum(c) OVER ()) AS f1
    FROM (SELECT predicted_rate AS r, count(*) AS n, count(*) FILTER (WHERE actual_cancelled) AS c
          FROM ontology.cancel_backtest WHERE period LIKE 'july_%' AND predicted_rate IS NOT NULL GROUP BY 1) g
    WINDOW w AS (ORDER BY r DESC)
) t ORDER BY f1 DESC, r DESC LIMIT 1)
SELECT round(100 * th.r, 1) AS threshold_pct,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r) AS flagged,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r AND b.actual_cancelled) AS flagged_cancelled,
       round(100.0 * count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r AND b.actual_cancelled)
             / nullif(count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r), 0), 1) AS precision_pct,
       round(100.0 * count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r AND b.actual_cancelled)
             / nullif(count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.actual_cancelled), 0), 1) AS recall_pct,
       round(2.0 * count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r AND b.actual_cancelled)
             / (count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r) + count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.actual_cancelled)), 3) AS f1
FROM ontology.cancel_backtest b CROSS JOIN th
WHERE b.period LIKE 'july_%' AND b.predicted_rate IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY th.r
```

</details>

WITH th AS (SELECT r FROM (
    SELECT r, 2.0 * sum(c) OVER w / (sum(n) OVER w + sum(c) OVER ()) AS f1

             / nullif(count(*) FILTER (WHERE b.predicted_rate >= th.r), 0), 1) AS precision_pct,

## Worked example: N102DN, July 19

The tail's next five scheduled legs after its 316-min DEN→ATL. Only ranks 1-3 are scored. BTS lists N102DN on both ATL→IAH (cancelled) and ATL→BWI (flown) at 02:50, so ATL→BWI, the leg it flew next, is rank 5.

| rank | flight_number | origin | dest | sched_dep | delay_bucket | stress_pct | predicted_pct | basis | cancelled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1487 | ATL | CLE | 07-19 18:08 | 240+ | 49.6 | 49.5 | carrier, 1309 samples | True |
| 2 | 1487 | CLE | ATL | 07-19 20:59 | 240+ | 49.6 | 51.4 | carrier, 1276 samples | True |

<details><summary>SQL</summary>

```sql
SELECT n.rank, s.flight_number, o.iata_code AS origin, d.iata_code AS dest,
       to_char(s.sched_dep_utc AT TIME ZONE 'UTC', 'MM-DD HH24:MI') AS sched_dep,
       b.delay_bucket, round(100 * b.stress, 1) AS stress_pct, round(100 * b.predicted_rate, 1) AS predicted_pct,
       coalesce(b.basis || ', ' || b.basis_samples || ' samples', 'not scored: rank ' || n.rank) AS basis,
       s.cancelled
FROM (SELECT x.*, row_number() OVER (ORDER BY x.sched_dep_utc, x.flight_id) AS rank
      FROM ontology.flight x JOIN ontology.flight i ON i.flight_id = (SELECT f.flight_id FROM ontology.flight f JOIN ontology.airport o ON o.airport_id = f.origin_airport_id
     WHERE f.tail_number = 'N102DN' AND f.flight_date = '2024-07-19' AND o.iata_code = 'DEN')
      WHERE x.tail_number = i.tail_number AND x.sched_dep_utc > i.sched_dep_utc
      ORDER BY x.sched_dep_utc, x.flight_id LIMIT 5) n
JOIN ontology.flight s ON s.flight_id = n.flight_id
JOIN ontology.airport o ON o.airport_id = s.origin_airport_id
JOIN ontology.airport d ON d.airport_id = s.dest_airport_id
LEFT JOIN ontology.cancel_backtest b ON b.inbound_flight_id = (SELECT f.flight_id FROM ontology.flight f JOIN ontology.airport o ON o.airport_id = f.origin_airport_id
     WHERE f.tail_number = 'N102DN' AND f.flight_date = '2024-07-19' AND o.iata_code = 'DEN') AND b.target_flight_id = s.flight_id
ORDER BY n.rank
```

</details>
